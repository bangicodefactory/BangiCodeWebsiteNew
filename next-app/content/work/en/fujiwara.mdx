## The client

Fujiwara is a Japanese restaurant in northern Morocco, known for precise technique and a tightly curated menu. The dining experience is intentional — small covers, high-quality ingredients, a considered atmosphere. The brand needed social media that reflected that same level of care rather than generic food photography.

## The problem

The restaurant's Instagram presence had fallen behind the quality of the in-room experience. Posts were infrequent, inconsistent in style, and not converting browsers into bookings. The team had no time to manage content production alongside daily service, and previous attempts at self-shooting produced results that didn't match the restaurant's visual identity.

The problem wasn't distribution — the platform was there. The problem was that the content didn't earn attention or communicate value to someone seeing the brand for the first time.

## What we did

We took over Fujiwara's full content presence: concept, shoot, edit, caption, and schedule. Each month we produce a content calendar built around the menu — hero dishes, behind-the-scenes prep, seasonal specials, and atmosphere shots that capture the room without staging it.

Video is the core format. Reels shot at the restaurant follow a consistent visual grammar: close detail on technique, ambient sound, minimal text. Still photography covers the hero shots used in story highlights and the profile grid. Caption tone is restrained and direct — no hashtag spam, no forced urgency.

## Outcome

Content output quadrupled from the sporadic pre-engagement rate. Engagement rate on Reels consistently outperforms the restaurant's baseline from before the retainer. The restaurant reports that new customers now regularly arrive having seen specific dishes on Instagram — content is doing the conversion work that previously relied entirely on word of mouth. The retainer has been renewed continuously since launch.
